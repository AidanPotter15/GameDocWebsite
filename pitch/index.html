One page pitch

Written to be posted. The questions at the bottom are the point of posting it.

Short version

A salvage game where your base is a machine you drive, the world is fixed and hand-made, and the thing that kills you is not being shot. It's what you're carrying going bad before you get anywhere with it.

The world

Post-collapse. Fixed terrain, so routes and distances can be learned and planned. Loot is procedural, so what's in a place changes between runs.

Nothing resets. No seasons, no wipes, no run-based progression.

Terrain and region layout are authored. Fully procedural interiors read as noise, and noise is why a lot of generated worlds feel empty.

The vessel

You have one machine. It carries you, your storage, and everything you've bolted onto it. It is not a real vehicle and doesn't pretend to be. The parts are invented, the failure rules are set by the design, and you can learn all of it by using it.

It's also the save file. After forty hours it's covered in mismatched parts pulled off dead things, and you remember where each one came from.

The survival system

Most games in this space make food a bar that ticks down. Here it's the whole problem.

What you salvage is perishable. It spoils on a clock, faster or slower depending on how you're storing it. Cold storage degrades with use and you can watch it happening. Fuel competes with cargo for the same hold space.

So the skill the game asks for is judgment, not reflexes:

  • Do I take the heavy haul I can't keep cold, or the light one that lasts?
  • Does the meat survive one more leg, or do I turn back now?
  • Do I spend hold space on fuel I can't sell to reach a place I can't otherwise get to?

A bad run doesn't cost you health. It costs you everything in the hold.

Why not another looter

The failure state is different. Combat games ask whether you can win a fight. This asks whether you understood what you were carrying.

What it isn't

  • Not voxel, not block building
  • No seasons, wipes, resets, or runs
  • Not a real place and not a real vehicle
  • Not a combat game with a hunger meter attached

Questions I want answered

Concept stage. Looking for holes before building anything.

  1. Does spoilage as a core mechanic sound like a game or like a chore? What would make it the first one?
  2. Has something already done this and I've missed it?
  3. Fixed terrain with procedural loot. Does exploration run dry, and how fast?
